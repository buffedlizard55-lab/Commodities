How prices are read Kalshi’s binary order book returns bids for YES and NO. A YES ask is derived as 1 − best NO bid, and a NO ask as 1 − best YES bid, as documented by Kalshi. Derived asks are labelled; a paper fill requires a fresh order-book response.

04 · research roster

Strategies under test

Every username has a falsifiable rule and an evidence gate. The roster aims at returns, not a risk-management score. A strategy is not allowed to create a trade until the data needed for its rule and the exchange price are both present.

05 · research method

Backtest only when the tape is real.

The project separates three states that are often mixed together: historical replay, live forward testing, and a research hypothesis. The UI never turns a hypothesis into a result.

01

Verify the event

Read the market ticker, status, close time, title, settlement terms and official API timestamp. A link to a third-party summary is not settlement evidence.

02

Verify the executable book

Fetch the open market’s order book, preserve the raw level prices and quantities, and consume depth level by level. A midpoint is a mark, not a fill.

03

Log the fill ledger

Store entry and exit times, side, VWAP, requested and filled size, level-by-level liquidity, slippage, fee assumption, source URL and market close time.

04

Explain the outcome

Leaderboard commentary is derived from observed fills. No fills means no return claim. The committed backtest uses only stored Kalshi candlesticks and official settlements, never reconstructed or invented bars; every future backtest must meet the same bar.

06 · audit ledger

Every placed simulation, readable

No records yet. A record is created only after a live API response or an intent generated from one.

Storage is local to this browser under a versioned schema. Export regularly if this record matters. Raw API snapshots are intentionally compact and capped; fill records retain the exact execution evidence needed for review.
